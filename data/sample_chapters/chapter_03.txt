The journey back to Ashenmoor took twice as long as the journey out. Kael's injured left arm forced them to move slowly, and Liora insisted on stopping every few hours to check the bandage. The wound was clean — the healing salve had done its work — but the muscle beneath was torn, and Kael could not grip anything with his left hand.

They arrived in Ashenmoor by midafternoon of the second day. The village elder, a weathered woman named Maren, met them at the gate. When Liora presented the crawler's severed head and the silver locket belonging to one of the lost shepherds, the elder's eyes filled with tears.

"You have done what no one else would," Maren said. She pressed a pouch of coin into Liora's hand — their agreed payment — and insisted they stay at the village inn to rest and recover.

At the inn, Liora returned the silver locket to the dead shepherd's widow. The woman wept and pressed a small token into Liora's palm — a carved bone amulet that had been in her family for generations. "For luck," the widow said. "You brought him home, in a way."

Kael spent the next three days resting his arm. The village healer examined it and confirmed what Liora already knew: the tendons were badly torn. He would heal, but it would take weeks, and until then his left arm was effectively useless. The healer fashioned a proper sling for him.

On the third evening, a traveling merchant passed through Ashenmoor. Kael sought him out and, after some haggling, traded Dawnfang — cracked crossguard and all — for a shorter, sturdier blade better suited to one-handed fighting. It pained him to part with the enchanted sword, but a weapon he could not wield properly was no weapon at all.

"You traded Dawnfang?" Liora asked, surprised, when he returned to the inn.

Kael shrugged with his good shoulder. "A sword is a tool. This one fits my hand as it is now." He held up a plain but well-forged short sword. "Besides, the merchant seemed to think a cracked enchanted blade was still worth more than a sound common one. I came out ahead."

Liora shook her head but said nothing. She spent the evening restocking her supplies from the village apothecary: two fresh vials of healing salve and a new pouch of flash powder, purchased with their payment from the elder.

By the fourth morning, they had decided to move on. Word had reached Ashenmoor of trouble in the mountain town of Craghollow to the east — miners reporting strange sounds deep underground. It was work, and they needed work.

They set out from Ashenmoor at dawn, heading east on the mountain road. Kael walked with his arm in a sling, his new short sword at his hip. Liora carried her restocked satchel and her journal, the bone amulet tucked safely inside. The Greymist Marshes were behind them. Whatever lay ahead, they would face it together.
