Dawn came grey and damp. Kael and Liora left the waystation lodge and waded into the Greymist Marshes, following a series of wooden stakes that marked an old path through the wetland. The water was knee-deep in places and bitterly cold.

By midday they found the first sign of the missing shepherds: a crook jammed upright in the mud, its owner nowhere to be seen. Nearby, Liora spotted tracks in the soft ground — not human, but something heavy that dragged itself on many legs.

"Marsh crawler," Liora whispered, crouching to examine the tracks. She sketched them quickly in her journal. "A big one. The flash powder might blind it long enough for you to get close."

They pressed deeper into the marsh. The mist thickened until they could barely see ten paces ahead. Then they heard it — a low, bubbling hiss from somewhere to their left.

The creature burst from the fog without warning. It was the size of a cart horse, a chitinous thing with too many legs and a maw ringed with hooked teeth. Kael drew Dawnfang and met the charge. The enchanted blade bit deep into the creature's carapace, spraying black ichor, but one of its barbed forelimbs raked across Kael's left arm, tearing through leather and flesh.

"Now!" Kael shouted through gritted teeth.

Liora hurled her entire pouch of flash powder into the creature's face. The explosion of light and heat sent it reeling backward, screeching. In that moment Kael drove Dawnfang through the gap between its head plates. The marsh crawler shuddered and collapsed into the water.

Kael sank to one knee, clutching his left arm. Blood ran freely between his fingers, and the wound was ragged and deep. Liora was at his side in moments, pressing one of her two healing salves into the gash. The bleeding slowed, but the arm would need proper rest.

"That's one salve gone," she said, binding the wound tightly with a strip torn from her cloak. "I have one left. Try not to need it."

Kael forced a grim laugh. He looked down at Dawnfang, its blade still slick with black ichor, and cleaned it on the marsh grass. The sword had served him well — but he noticed a crack running along the crossguard. The impact had damaged it.

They searched the crawler's nest and found the remains of the three shepherds. There was nothing to bring back but a silver locket one of them had worn, which Liora pocketed to return to the village. She also used her hempen rope — borrowed from Kael's pack — to drag the creature's severed head free as proof of the kill.

As they limped back toward the waystation, Kael handed Liora his tinderbox. "Hold onto this," he said. "I can barely grip anything with this arm."

They reached the lodge by nightfall, exhausted but alive. Kael's left arm was badly injured, and Liora had used one of her two healing salves. Their inventory was lighter: no more flash powder, and the rope was fouled with crawler ichor beyond salvage. But they had the proof they needed.
